Tax assumptions

Rates applied to boot, to the "if you'd sold instead" comparison, and to the taxable part of DST distributions. State tax and the NIIT are added on top of each federal rate.

Depreciation recapture is recognized first (up to the lesser of accumulated depreciation or total taxable boot), taxed at the recapture rate; any remaining boot is taxed at the capital gains rate above. State tax and the NIIT, if included, are added to both rates.

Fiduciary

This tool illustrates how DST return mechanics work. It is not investment, tax, or legal advice. Its tax figures are rough estimates: it models federal capital-gains tax, optionally unrecaptured Section 1250 depreciation recapture, a single flat state rate, and optionally the 3.8% net investment income tax, all at rates you supply and simply added together. It does not model local tax, the deductibility of state tax, the income thresholds that decide whether the NIIT applies, passive-activity or at-risk limits, distribution timing, sponsor-specific fees beyond the two modeled here, or the terms of any actual offering.

The default per-category return figures are illustrative placeholders, not researched market data and not a forecast. Consult a qualified tax advisor and read the offering documents before making any investment decision. Nothing here carries any guarantee of accuracy.
